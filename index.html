Planck's law, drawn per unit wavelength and scaled to its own peak; the marker sits at Wien's λmax = 2.898 mm·K ÷ T. Counted per unit frequency instead, the peak lands 1.76× lower, which is why the cosmic microwave background is quoted both at 1.06 mm and at 160 GHz: the same glow, sliced two ways. Radiated power is Stefan-Boltzmann, σT⁴, for a perfect emitter.

Even the wind is sunlight

All wind is reprocessed sunlight. The Sun delivers energy to Earth purely as electromagnetic radiation. It lands unevenly: equator against poles, land against sea, day against night. The uneven heating makes pressure differences, and air falls down those gradients. Roughly 1 to 2% of incoming solar power ends up as wind. So every gust is EM energy that arrived as photons, thermalized, and got converted into moving air. A wind turbine is, with a couple of steps in between, solar powered.

And we measure wind with EM. Doppler weather radar bounces microwaves off raindrops and reads the frequency shift of the echo: moving droplets compress or stretch the returning wave, and that shift is the wind speed. Lidar does the same trick with laser light off dust and aerosols; satellite scatterometers read ocean wind from how microwaves scatter off wind-roughened water. Wind has no EM signature of its own, so we interrogate it with EM and read the answer in the Doppler shift.
