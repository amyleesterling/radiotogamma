Deck 04 · the Gamma Attic

The top floor is 35 octaves tall.

Gamma ray is not a band with two edges. It is the open-ended top of the spectrum: everything above the x-rays, however high. From its doorstep near 100 keV to the most energetic photon ever caught, the gamma "band" spans more than ten orders of magnitude, wider than any other named band and about as wide as radio, microwave and infrared put together. The ship has laid it out as a ladder. Climb.

Why the attic looks empty

Nature's known accelerators, pulsar winds, supernova remnants, the jets of feeding black holes and the winds of giant star clusters, seem to top out near PeV photon energies. Above about 100 TeV the universe itself censors the view: over intergalactic distances, gamma rays annihilate against the infrared background and then against the cosmic microwave background itself. So the highest floors are structurally sound but unvisited: allowed by the physics, unreached by the sources, and hidden by the sky.

One PeV photon carries about 1.6 × 10-4 joules: the kinetic energy of a ping-pong ball rolling across a table at a third of a metre per second, packed into a single quantum of light. And the attic does not stop at the record: between 2.5 PeV and the Planck energy lie almost thirteen more orders of magnitude in which no photon has ever been seen.
